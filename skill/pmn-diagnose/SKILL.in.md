# Diagnosing a case with PMN

A diagnosis without evidence is an opinion with PMN's vocabulary. PMN supplies the questions and the tests; the
facts come from the user, from sources, or are clearly marked as assumptions. The value of the output is that
someone can check it: every finding has evidence, every claim about PMN has a section id.

## First, the level

PMN analyses at three levels that run at once (§7.0b): the **organism** level (the conditions of particular people),
the **interaction and organisation** level (how people behave toward each other; institutions and their rules), and
the **system** level (resource distributions, power configurations and information environments that shape both).
Scale itself is a variable (§7.0c).

Before anything else, decide which level is *primary in producing the outcome*. §7.0b calls the cross-level error
the most persistent one: explaining a system outcome by one person's character, or a personal failure by "the
system" when it really was individual. Most real cases need two levels; say which is primary and why.

## Sections to read

Always: §7.0b, §6.2 and §6.3 (judge by incentives and structural position, not stated intentions), §12.1b
(second-order effects, including of doing nothing), §12.5d (misuse triggers, run on your own draft) and §12.5e
(partial data). Then by level:

- **Micro (organism, household, workplace, community).** §3.4 the minimal anchor (biological floor) and §3.4b the
  typology of suffering; §5.6 and §5.6b meaning as a material condition; §3.12 how individually rational choices
  add up to collective problems; §10.15 micro-politics and structural change.
- **Meso (organisation, institution, policy).** §7.3c-i the five-stage capture sequence and §7.3b its early-warning
  indicators; §7.3c incentive architecture; §6.5 how institutions preserve themselves; §12.5 (capture diagnostics
  apply regardless of ideology); §6.6 when legitimacy is involved.
- **Macro (sector, country, regime, international order).** §15.2 and §15.4 transformation pressure
  T = S × D × P × G, with §15.0b for the canonical terms and §15.13 for what the formulas cannot do; §15.12 reading
  current conditions; §10.5 thresholds and §10.5b political time; §7.8 regime types; §9.0 and §9.2 for the
  international dimension.
- Remedies, only if asked: §7.3d for institutions, §10.6 and §10.9 for system change. §13.1c when someone calls a
  long-standing conflict permanent.

This list is where to start, not a boundary: when a section you read points to another that bears on the case,
open that one too.

## Procedure

1. **Scope.** Name the situation, the period and who is affected. If facts are thin, ask for them or list what you
   are assuming. Keep three kinds of statement apart: facts the user gave, facts from your general knowledge (flag
   these, and note they may be out of date), and inferences.
2. **Level.** Primary level and secondary level, with the reason (see above).
3. **Material arrangement.** Who funds or supplies it, who decides, who bears the costs, who gains. PMN requires the
   distribution to be stated (§12.3b, the distribution visibility test), so do not stop at "stakeholders".
4. **Apply the level's tests.**
   - Micro: which conditions touch the biological floor, which touch meaning, and which are produced by the
     structure around the person rather than by the person.
   - Meso: for each of the five capture stages, the evidence for and against, using the §7.3b indicators. The case
     sits at the highest stage with real evidence.
   - Macro: evidence for each factor of T (S, D, P, G) and for the institution's adaptive and repressive capacity;
     where the formulas stop being informative, say so (§15.13).
5. **Consequences.** First-order and second-order, across the layers in §12.1b, and the consequences of inaction.
6. **Check your own draft (§12.5d).** Does the conclusion conveniently favour the most powerful actor? Is the
   suffering of the least visible population missing? Did the user's framing already contain the conclusion? If a
   trigger fires, say so and adjust.
7. **Data gaps (§12.5e).** Name what is missing and why it might be missing. Give the strength of each inference in
   the form "supported at [strength] given [assumptions]; reversed by [evidence]".
8. **Remedies, only if asked,** aimed at the primary level: an organisational problem needs organisational
   redesign, a system problem does not yield to replacing individuals (§7.0b).

## Rules

- Do not overclaim. A stage, a factor or a floor violation needs its evidence; where the evidence stops, say so,
  even if the case feels worse.
- Treat applications symmetrically: the same tests whatever the actor's ideology and whatever you or the user think
  of it (§12.5). On live political controversies give the structure and the evidence, not a partisan verdict.
- At the micro level PMN explains the structure around a person's situation. It is not therapy or personal advice:
  say what the structural reading explains, what it cannot, and do not turn a personal question into a verdict on
  the person.
- If the text is silent on something the case needs, say so rather than inventing a PMN position.

## Output

1. **Diagnosis:** one paragraph, plain language.
2. **Level:** primary and secondary, and why.
3. **Facts used:** given / general knowledge / assumed.
4. **Findings for the level:** for meso cases a table with columns Stage, Evidence, Status (present / absent /
   unknown); for macro cases a table with columns Factor, Evidence, Strength; for micro cases the floor, meaning and
   structure findings.
5. **Who pays, who gains.**
6. **Second-order effects,** including of inaction.
7. **Confidence and what would overturn it:** specific, observable evidence.
8. **Sections read.**

Answer in the user's language; keep section ids and quoted sentences in the original English.
