# PMN v126: reading paths

The reading paths on the PMN website (https://novadharma-hub.github.io/pmn-framework/), each a short sequence for a particular reader.

## 01. Epistemic Foundations & Realism

For: Philosophers & Academic Researchers. About 40 min, 4 sections.

Ground your inquiry in mind-independent material realism, physicalist limits, and the biological floor of suffering before engaging doctrine or applied cases.

1. §2.1 Reality as Primary: Mind-independent physical reality as the primary ground.
2. §1.9 Epistemic Authority: How Claims Acquire Social Standing: Who gets believed without checking: epistemic authority as an unequally distributed material resource.
3. §2.4 Social Ontology: The Reality of Emergent Structures: Thermodynamic, structural, and agent tiers without reductive collapse.
4. §3.4 The Minimal Anchor: Sentient vulnerability and non-negotiable suffering minimization.

## 02. Power Forensics & Anti-Capture

For: Policy Analysts & Institutional Auditors. About 50 min, 4 sections.

Dissect how power entrenches, how information asymmetries are leveraged, and how protective institutions degenerate into predatory extraction apparatuses.

1. §6.2 Power as Structural Position: Material disparities that construct uneven bargaining fields.
2. §7.3 The Custodian Problem: How protective stewardship mutates into self-preserving interest.
3. §7.3c-i A General Theory of Capture: Mechanism, Stages, and Detection: The canonical account of capture: mechanism, stages, and detection.
4. §8.4b Narrative Typology: How Governing Narratives Structure Political Time: How governing narratives structure political time and what populations can perceive as possible.

## 03. Compressed Core (Fast-Track)

For: Quick Readers & AI Context Briefing. About 25 min, 4 sections.

The most compressed formulation of PMN’s core thesis, diagnostic power mechanics, and materialist ethics in under thirty minutes.

1. §15.15 The Compressed Core: PMN Without the Architecture: The definitive, high-density summary of PMN metaphysics and ethics.
2. §1.9 Epistemic Authority: How Claims Acquire Social Standing: How claims acquire social standing, and why that standing is a resource distributed unequally.
3. §3.4 The Minimal Anchor: The non-arbitrary biological floor of moral valuation.
4. §7.3 The Custodian Problem: Methods for auditing custodian advantage and capture signatures.

## 04. Applied Ethics, Agency & Becoming

For: Existential Readers & Ethical Practitioners. About 45 min, 3 sections.

Move from the biological floor of suffering prevention toward the evaluative ceiling of human flourishing (genuine becoming) and historical praxis.

1. §3.4 The Minimal Anchor: The minimal anchor: reducing structural suffering, and why the floor is not arbitrary.
2. §4.5 Beyond the Minimal Anchor: The Anti-Foreclosure Criterion: Optimal developmental capacity expansion as the evaluative ceiling.
3. §17.1 The Prohibition Case: When Moral Clarity Produces Structural Harm: Moral action and systemic trade-offs under material resource constraints.

## 05. Situation Diagnostics & Field Audit

For: Systemic Reformers & Strategists. About 55 min, 4 sections.

Deploy PMN analytical instruments to measure transformation pressure and formulate tactical interventions in real-world institutions.

1. §2.4 Social Ontology: The Reality of Emergent Structures: Separating thermodynamic limits, structural incentives, and individual agency.
2. §15.2 The Primary Formula: The net force pushing a system toward reorganization: T = S × D × P × G.
3. §7.3 The Custodian Problem: Empirical audit indicators for institutional integrity degradation.
4. §11.0 Two Levels of Economic Analysis: Keeps two levels apart: the questions any economic analysis must ask, and PMN's own economic doctrine.

## 06. Economic Doctrine & Contestability

For: Political Economists & Policy Designers. About 45 min, 4 sections.

Transcend ownership formalism toward contestable governance, surplus allocation auditing, and unconditional biological floor security.

1. §11.1 Economics Without Prior Commitments: Economic systems are tools; which works is an empirical question, not a prior commitment.
2. §11.3 Horizon and Instrument: The horizon the minimal anchor points toward, versus the instruments that move toward it now.
3. §11.5 Power Beyond Ownership: Expertise, Information, and Network Effects: Power that does not come from ownership: expertise, information, and network effects.
4. §3.10 Ecological Constraints: The Ground Beneath All Systems: Biospheric carrying capacity and thermodynamic limits on production.
